All figures come from spatial block cross-validation: each zone was scored by a model that never saw it.

Threshold-free reference

These do not change with k: they score the whole ranking. k only decides how much ground you act on, which is the trade-off this tab compares.

Favorable zone vs. block probability

Favorable zone ranks every 1.5 km cell by model score and marks the best k% of the area. It answers “where should we look first?”. Its numbers are relative: share of known mines and gold that fall in the marked area, and how many times better than random (lift). It makes no claim about the chance of a discovery in a given cell.

Block probability groups cells into blocks (4.5, 10.5 or 15 km) and turns the block score into a probability with isotonic regression, fitted on other folds: “what fraction of blocks like this one hold at least one known mine?”. Its numbers are absolute and can be checked: among the blocks marked here, the share that really holds a mine (hit rate) should match the mean calibrated probability.

Same model, same ranking: the block view does not reorder the map, it adds a calibrated, checkable chance at a scale where a cell-level probability would be meaninglessly small.

Mines: project inventory (136 points). Circle size: contained gold of the USGS deposit (past production, reserves and resources; USGS Open-File Report 2014-1074). USGS reports one figure per deposit, not per mine: when a deposit groups several inventory mines, its gold is drawn once, at its main mine, and the other mines are shown as small points. Model cells are 1.5 km. Access and environment layer: project traffic-light map (green, yellow, red), read at 300 m on the map and as the most frequent class of each 1.5 km cell in the figures. Basemaps: Esri World Light Gray Canvas and Esri World Imagery with reference labels.
